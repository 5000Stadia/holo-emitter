# Facts for the production-system decision (2026-10-05)

These are facts only, each with its source. The plan is deliberately left out.

## What the person wants (their words)

- **The goal.** "Describe a world, live render the world." The far test (now in the Vision, ROADMAP.md): "throw the Alice in Wonderland story at pattern buffer and have holo emitter ingest it in such a way, that I can play a 3-D interactive world based on the book." (lab/PRODUCTION-NOTES.md)
- **Where AI belongs.** "a rigorous rule set and separation of object types, and behaviors to allow for many shapes of structures", using AI "so we don't have to dwell too much in the nondeterministic intelligence". (same)
- **How AI is spent.** Item characteristics are bound to the location; "when the item is being retrieved, the initial structure of its presence in text is ingested one time zero additional LLM calls and from that point it's unique structure is then interpreted for best execution". (conversation, 2026-10-05)
- **The furthest form.** "a web hosted engine … with an endlessly curating library of objects from any situation than any user engages with such that a whole world could potentially be made with only one or two assets, not having already been rendered, and those one or two assets upon render immediately join the library server side". (PRODUCTION-NOTES item 9)
- **Reuse with variety.** Sub-components are objects of their own, reused. The risk he named: a library filled with one title. His fix: no titles in the catalogue; live text in a text slot. (item 4)
- **Context shapes contents.** "The fullness or emptiness of a bookshelf, desk or refrigerator is likely notably different between someone in squalor and one who is wealthy." (item 11)
- **Influences, not only owners.** Ownership generalises to the influences that shape an area, including factions, for streets and hillsides. (item 12)
- **The rest of his inputs (items 2, 3, 6, 7, 10):**
  - ambient animation by state (lit/cold fire, a stoplight);
  - for the proof of concept, worlds without living creatures (characters, if any, as a 2D overlay);
  - takeable decided from size;
  - ground that isn't flat, and outdoor landforms ("Majestic Hills");
  - streaming: "fully rendered before load being your room and each connected space from that location, and 2 locations away in every direction being the live work".
- **Standing ruling.** No image generation; code-only. (ROADMAP R32, on hold; memory "asset-pipeline-direction")

## Measured on what exists

The machine has no GPU and renders with SwiftShader. Sources are lab/RECEIPTS.md and lab/house/TIMING.md.

| What | Measured |
|---|---|
| Room from a written brief (strongroom) | Compile 10–18 ms. Build 0.43 s, rising to 1.38 s with two 1024² textures made at build. Material kit 2.9–4 s once per look. Light bake (75 probes, 2 bounces) 27–33 s here. Walkable at 5–6 s without the bake. |
| Authoring cost per new piece | Strongroom 19 min (7 new pieces). Bookpress 9 min (research 1 min, piece 1.2 min, checking 6 min). Book as its own recipe 5 min. Titles and context 4.5 min. Owners and trinkets 5 min. Checking was about two-thirds of authoring time, at about 40 s per render here. |
| Size of a recipe vs its result | Bookpress recipe 16 KB (6 KB gzip). The same object exported as GLB is 11.8 MB: 7 MB geometry (54k triangles, about 40k of them carving) plus textures. |
| Instancing | One unit book drawn 581 times, each with its own matrix, colour, spine cell and title cell. Library wall: 0.12 s, 3 meshes, 80 KB geometry. "great" vs "poor" context on the same wall: 600 vs 273 books, both 0.16 s. |
| Whole house from a generated plan (111 rooms, 3 floors) | Compile 9 ms. Rooms about 147 ms each (16.3 s total). Walkable in 4.7 s with nearest rooms first. Peak about 1,000 draws, 1.6M triangles, heap about 255 MB in the great hall. |
| Build thread | Every build runs on the main thread today. |

## Faults found, and their causes (lab/RECEIPTS.md)

| Fault | Cause |
|---|---|
| Books scattered with gaps on a poor shelf | The arrangement rule was written for variety, not from how people keep books. The person caught it. |
| Sliver stones on a floor | A tiling texture whose joints didn't wrap at the tile edge. The person caught it. |
| Ochre "limewash" | A material reused from another period's look. |
| Blank book spines | A shared per-member variation helper shifted UVs on atlas-mapped parts. |
| A press behind its neighbour in a corner | No clearance rule. Fixed with a check that fails on the old build. |
| Rolls intersecting in pigeonholes | Random placement in place of packing. |

## Prior art in the person's own projects

- **pattern-buffer (archived).** Blind AI extractors agree on things a text names, but diverge on derived constructs. The remedy that worked was "a declared entity space, kind-level … a short list of canonical ids handed to every sensor". In their three-run test, base attributes dropped from 266 to 29 distinct. (Archive&Dead/Pattern Buffer Evolution/PORTABLE-FINDINGS.md §1)
- **projector (archived).** The store records assertions with provenance and resolves nothing; the projector composes answers under a named, swappable policy. (Archive&Dead/projector/ARCHITECTURE.md §1)
- **holo-emitter's own core.** One world document is the truth, and the picture is its projection. Relations (in, held_by) and knowledge gate what exists on screen. The harness takes intents. (design/intention.md; src/harness.js; tests code-room.spec.mjs)

## Prior art outside: Worldspring (github.com/Dun-John/worldspring, used as reference by the person's leave)

- Every artifact is a pure function of (world file, job key). Dependencies point only to coarser levels or parent features. (crates/worldgen/src/pipeline/mod.rs)
- Samples are a function of a global lattice index, so seams agree. (its CLAUDE.md, Rules)
- Building function → archetype → room program per level → furnishing per room kind. (crates/worldgen/src/interior/mod.rs)
- Sketch constraints are honoured, or reported as conflicts. (t0/sketch.rs)
- Edits sit outside the world hash. A generator version is pinned per world, and old generators are kept. (world.rs; app/src/world/versions.ts)
- Generation runs in workers; there's a performance floor on a reference device. (its CLAUDE.md)

## Constraints

- **Platform:** a browser (desktop and phone) on three.js r160 and WebGL2. No server today; a static LAN server.
- **People:** a solo developer, with weekly usage limits on the agents.
- **Code style:** recipes are JavaScript modules today. Kinds and their parts are functions in `lab/painted/procedural.js`, `lab/brief/*.js` and `lab/house/*.js`.
