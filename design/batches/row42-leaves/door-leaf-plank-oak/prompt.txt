Paint one object on its own: an English oak plank door leaf of about 1660, hung on iron strap hinges.

This is a SINGLE OBJECT and not a picture of a room. There is no wall,
  no doorway, no floor, no room behind it, no furniture, no hand, no
  person, no landscape, no sky. The leaf and its ironwork, and nothing
  else.

Ground: fully transparent. The leaf is cut out to its own edge with
  clean alpha, no background colour, no card, no drop shadow, no
  reflection, no vignette, no border. It will be placed into a doorway
  someone else painted, and anything drawn around it would sit on top
  of that doorway.

Viewpoint: square on, looking straight at the face of the leaf from
  directly in front of it, as if standing in the doorway. No
  perspective, no vanishing point, no convergence, no tilt. The face
  is parallel to the picture plane, so a plank joint runs straight and
  keeps the same width from top to bottom. The leaf fills the image
  edge to edge with no margin on any side.

Light: completely even and sourceless. No sun, no window light, no
  firelight, no candle, no directional key, no highlight, no cast
  shadow, no gradient from one side to the other, no bloom. The same
  brightness at the top as at the bottom and at both edges. This leaf
  will be lit later by the room it is hung in, so any light painted
  into it is light in the wrong place forever.

The construction, and it is exact where it says exact: vertical oak
  boards, five or six of them, butted or lightly V-jointed, running the
  full height. Two full-width wrought iron ledges (strap hinges) across
  the face — one about a quarter down, one about a quarter up from the
  bottom. THE HINGE SIDE IS THE VIEWER'S LEFT and it must be readable
  as such: each strap ends on the left in a broadened hinge plate and a
  pintle knuckle at the very edge of the leaf. A single wrought iron
  ring pull with its boss is on the RIGHT, at about hand height. No
  lock plate, no letter slot, no numbers, no knocker.

Proportion: roughly 0.9 m wide by 2.0 m tall, so a little over twice as
  tall as it is wide. It does not have to be exact — the leaf is scaled
  to whatever doorway it is placed in.

Medium: fine oil realism with tactile brush detail, the material's own
  colour, honest to English joinery of about 1660. Age and wear are
  welcome in the SURFACE — worn, scrubbed, weathered, the boards
  shrunken a little apart, the iron dark and pitted — but not as
  staining that reads as a shadow.

Avoid: any room, any doorway, any wall, any floor, any background of
  any kind, any perspective, any directional light, any cast shadow,
  any border or frame or margin, any caption, any colour chart, any
  watermark. The leaf and nothing else.
