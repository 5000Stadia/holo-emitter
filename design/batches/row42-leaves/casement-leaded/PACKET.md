# casement-leaded — the casement that goes in a detected window light

**What it replaces.** `src/placeholders.js`'s `casement-leaded-v1`, procedural
placeholder art that says so on its own record (`placeholder: true`,
`provenance.painter_ask` pointing here). Row 42 fits a casement to the rectangle
`window_measure.py` reads off the wall's own painting, so this sprite arrives
into a job that is already working.

**Transparent ground, and one thing more: the GLASS is transparent too.** The
painting behind this sprite has already drawn a leaded light with the daylight in
it. A casement that arrives with an opaque pane replaces that light with a grey
slab, which is the sticker the flip test exists to catch. The came lattice and
the ironwork are opaque; the quarries between them are clear, with at most the
faintest wash of old green glass.

**Neutral light** — row 37's rule [HUMAN, 2026-08-24, verbatim: *"all panels meed
to have no light source and there should be a light lighting shader over the top
regarding light sources"*]. This supersedes the orientation contract's `UL45` key
for every asset generated from here on.

**A readable hinge side, on the viewer's LEFT,** for the same reason the leaf has
one: the casement swings to it. Hinges and pins on the left, the fastener — a
cockspur or a ring — on the right.

**Proportion.** The plan rules every window in this manor with its sill at 0.90 m
and its head at 2.00 m, and the lights run 1.40–1.50 m across, so draw the
casement at roughly 1.4 m × 1.1 m — WIDER THAN IT IS TALL, which is what the
paintings show (`kitchen/E`'s measured light is 233 × 192 px). The renderer
scales it to whatever light it is placed in.

Second state (`casement swung inward, close to edge-on`) is a follow-up ask and
NOT part of this one: the placeholder's own open state ships until then.
