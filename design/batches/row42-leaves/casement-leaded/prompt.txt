Paint one object on its own: an English leaded casement of about 1660 — a hinged iron window frame filled with diamond quarries of leaded glass.

This is a SINGLE OBJECT and not a picture of a room or of a window in a
  wall. There is no wall, no stone surround, no reveal, no sill, no
  mullion, no room behind it, no view through it, no curtain, no
  landscape, no sky. The casement and nothing else.

Ground: fully transparent, AND THE GLASS IS TRANSPARENT TOO. The lead
  cames and the iron frame are solid; every diamond quarry between them
  is clear, with at most the faintest wash of old green glass. There is
  a painted window behind this sprite carrying its own daylight, and an
  opaque pane would blot it out. No background colour, no card, no drop
  shadow, no reflection, no vignette, no border.

Viewpoint: square on, looking straight at the casement from directly in
  front of it, as if standing in the room. No perspective, no vanishing
  point, no convergence, no tilt. The frame is parallel to the picture
  plane, so a came runs straight and keeps the same width across the
  whole light. The casement fills the image edge to edge with no margin
  on any side.

Light: completely even and sourceless. No sun, no daylight streaming
  through, no glare, no directional key, no highlight, no cast shadow,
  no gradient from one side to the other, no bloom. This casement will
  be lit later by the room and the daylight behind it, so any light
  painted into it is light in the wrong place forever.

The construction, and it is exact where it says exact: a wrought iron
  casement frame — four flat bars, head, sill and two stiles — filled
  with small diamond quarries in a lead came grid, each quarry roughly
  0.10 to 0.15 m across, the same size and the same angle over the whole
  light. THE HINGE SIDE IS THE VIEWER'S LEFT and it must be readable as
  such: two or three iron hinges and their pins on the left stile,
  standing at the very edge. On the RIGHT stile, a wrought iron
  fastener — a cockspur handle or a ring — at about hand height. No
  heraldry, no coloured glass, no painted roundel, no inscription.

Proportion: roughly 1.4 m wide by 1.1 m tall, so a little WIDER than it
  is tall. It does not have to be exact — the casement is scaled to
  whatever window light it is placed in.

Medium: fine oil realism with tactile brush detail, the material's own
  colour, honest to English glazing of about 1660. Age and wear are
  welcome in the SURFACE — the lead dulled and buckled a little, the
  iron dark and pitted, the glass slightly uneven — but not as staining
  that reads as a shadow.

Avoid: any wall, any stone surround, any room, any view through the
  glass, any background of any kind, any perspective, any directional
  light, any cast shadow, any border or frame or margin beyond the
  casement's own iron, any caption, any colour chart, any watermark.
  The casement and nothing else.
