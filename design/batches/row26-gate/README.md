## What changed, in one sentence

The standpoint law used to answer one question — how far back from a wall do you stand — and it
now answers two. The second is *where along the wall*: when a doorway the plan draws on a facing
runs off the frame from the room's centre, the body slides along its own wall by the least whole
centimetre that brings the doorway wholly into frame, clamped to floor it can actually stand on,
and refusing honestly when nothing satisfies it. Two of the manor's 88 facings move: `hall/N` by
0.93 m and `hall/S` by 1.43 m. Nothing else in the building moved a millimetre.

**The lens did not move** (one lens, 1024 px focal, as you approved at `02b`). **The eye did not
move** (the standing eye, 1.183 m, camera "B"). **The camera did not turn** — this is your body
taking a step sideways along a passage wall, not a pan. **No room gained a second standpoint**;
the great hall and the long gallery are still where that idea waits.
