Paint one flat, square sample of a single building material: heavy smoke-darkened oak ceiling joists with plain boards between them.

This is a MATERIAL SAMPLE and not a picture of a room. There is no
  room, no wall meeting another wall, no corner, no floor line, no
  ceiling, no window, no door, no furniture and no horizon. The
  ceiling surface fills the whole image, edge to edge.

Viewpoint: square on, looking straight at the surface from directly
  in front of it. No perspective, no vanishing point, no convergence,
  nothing receding. The surface is parallel to the picture plane, so a
  straight joint runs straight and stays the same width across the
  whole image.

Light: completely even and sourceless. No sun, no window light, no
  firelight, no lamp, no highlight, no cast shadow, no vignette, no
  gradient from one side to the other. The same brightness in every
  corner as in the middle. This sample will be lit later by the scene
  it is used in, so any light painted into it is light in the wrong
  place forever.

Scale, and this is exact: exactly 5 exposed joists lie side by side across the image, each one exactly 0.900 m wide measured across it.
  They run the other way — along their own length — from one edge of the image to the other, unbroken.
  So the image spans 4.500 m across. Every exposed joist is the same width as every other, and the count must be exact: it is measured off the returned image and a miscount is a re-ask.

Medium: fine oil realism with tactile brush detail, the material's own
  colour, honest to English building work of about 1660. Age and wear
  are welcome in the SURFACE — worn, scrubbed, weathered, settled — but
  not as staining that reads as a shadow.

Avoid: any room, any object, any perspective, any directional light,
  any border or frame or margin, any caption, any colour chart, any
  watermark. The material and nothing else.
