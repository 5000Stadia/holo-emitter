# The scored pass — §7.4, and it did not come out the way the row wanted

The row's plan froze six criteria **before anything was changed** and required a fresh comparator,
with no knowledge of the project, to score both versions unlabelled and randomised before the row
closes — with the rule stated in advance: *"A criterion the new frames lose is a finding, not a
note."*

Two comparators ran. **They reached opposite verdicts**, and this file records both, because
reporting only the one that agrees with the build would be the exact failure the rule exists to
prevent.

Each was given eight before frames and eight after frames, set labels randomised, facing order
shuffled, told nothing about which was newer, and forbidden from reading the repository.

## Run 1 — against the frames of 2026-08-21 (superseded)

| criterion | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| 1. standing in vs facing a wall | 4.00 | 4.00 |
| 2. where the room ends | **5.00** | 3.88 |
| 3. objects sit on the floor | 4.00 | **5.00** |
| 4. looks the size it is | 2.00 | **4.25** |
| 5. one body turning | 2.00 | **3.50** |
| 6. place, not diagram | 2.25 | **3.25** |
| overall | 3.21 | **4.00** |

Verdict: ship the after set. One criterion lost — **where the room ends**, −1.13, *entirely* on
`hall/N` and `hall/S`, which it scored 1. Its reason for shipping: the before set draws every far
wall at 96 px/m regardless of distance, so the camera re-zooms on every facing and distance is not
encoded in anything the eye reads first.

**These frames no longer exist.** They predated the facing-glyph resize and the `hall/E`
recomposition, which is its own finding and is in the row's record.

**The after set loses all six**, by 2.00, 1.87, 1.62, 1.38, 1.37 and 0.50. Verdict: ship the before

that goes blind on 25% of the passage's facings is not shippable."*

## What survives from both, and what does not

**The replicated finding, and it is the row's real look failure.** Both comparators, independently,
put the weight on `hall/N` and `hall/S` — the passage's two long walls, seen across a 2.60 m room,
which show no corner, no floor line and no ceiling line. Run 1 scored them 1 and lost one criterion
by them; run 2 scored them 1 across four criteria and lost everything by them. This is the row's own
named residue arriving from the outside, twice, unprompted. It is not a measurement artifact and it
is not a matter of weighting: **two of eight facings tell a viewer nothing about the room they are
standing in.**

**Run 2 contradicts itself on criterion 4, and the contradiction matters.** It scored the BEFORE set
4.25 on *"does the room look the size it is"* while observing, in its own report, that the before
set's *"far wall is 269 px tall in every single frame regardless of whether the wall is 2.6 m or
12 m away."* A camera that draws every wall the same size whatever its distance cannot convey size;
that is the defect this row removed, and it is scored as a virtue. Run 1 scored the same property
2.00 and called it out. The two runs disagree on criterion 4 by 3.24 points in opposite directions,
and only one of them is consistent with its own observations.

**Run 2's lens arithmetic is wrong by a factor of two, and it drives several of its conclusions.**
It derives a 41.4° horizontal field and an eye 7.7–12.0 m from the wall it faces. The build's lens
is 73.7° (f = 1024 px on a 1536 frame, the row's whole subject) and its standpoints run 2.15 m to
6.00 m — exactly half its figures. Everything it infers *from the lens* — "the lens is simply too
long", "at 41° the frame is a crop of the last third of the room", the candlestick "crushed into
67 px" — rests on that error. What it measured directly is exact: wall widths 2.594 / 4.797 /
5.446 / 7.996 m against the true 2.60 / 4.80 / 5.45 / 8.00, ceiling 2.80 m, and both eye heights.

## The four things a blind eye found that no criterion asked for

1. **The eye height reads as a child's.** Unprompted, and measured correctly at 1.09 m: *"M's viewer
   is crouching or is a child, and it makes every M wall tower."* This bears directly on the eye
   fork that was resolved by delegation — it is the first outside opinion on the measured camera,
   and it is not a friendly one.
2. **The camera is outside the room in both versions**, so the floor is never at the viewer's feet —
   nearest visible floor 2.08 m in the before set, and the after set's own 2.23 m. Both comparators
   said a version of this. It is the intention's fifth quality, and it is still open.
3. **Objects exist only on the wall you face.** Turn 90° in the study and the furniture vanishes.
   Both runs found it; it predates this row.
4. **The candlestick still first-reads as standing ON the press**, in both versions, and the foot
   dropping below the plinth is doing all the work alone. The row moved that pair from 7 px of base
   separation to 68 px after run 1 flagged it, and run 2 — looking at the moved version — says the
   read is now "in front", but by the weakest cue in the frame.

## What this file is for

Nothing here is settled by an agent. The row's rule was that a lost criterion is a finding, and six
were lost; the honest state is that **the two rooms are geometrically true and one of two independent
outside eyes still prefers the version that was geometrically false**, because the true version goes
blind on two facings and the false one never does. That is the question the batch carries.
