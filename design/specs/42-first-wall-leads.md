# 42 — parts (1), (2) and (3)

Parts (1) and (2) landed first and are below. Part (3) — the placed leaf and casement sprites — is
the section at the end.

## (1) The first wall leads, everywhere

**Where the choice lives.** `tools/edge-seed.mjs`, beside `openOrder`, because that file already
owns "which facing continues which" and a second home for it would drift. It gains
`carrierLoad`, `entryDoorFacing`, `leadFacing` and `roomOrder`; `openOrder` becomes the open
location's view of `roomOrder` rather than a second ring.

**The lead.** The most-carried facing of the room — doors, windows and fireplaces counted off
`facingCarriers` (`open_edge` is not a carrier a wall shows). Ties break to the wall the plan's
entry door faces: the room's lowest-ordered `plan.openings` door is its entry, the wall it stands
in is the wall you come through, and the wall that door *faces* is the one opposite. Ties that
survive that break to compass order, which is what a room with no door has anyway.

**The order.** Indoors it is a star: the lead is position 0 and the other three continue the lead.
On an OPEN location it stays row 38's ring — that row's sequential requirement is [HUMAN] and
unrevoked, and it is the only construction that gives the fourth facing a seam neighbour — but the
ring is re-origined at the lead instead of at compass order. Either way the lead is painted first
and the other three wait for it, which is what the row asks.

**What a dependent ask carries.** Image 1 is the LEAD's picture (row 40's own-room rule, extended
to accept the lead's CANDIDATE where the lead is not promoted yet — guarded, so a lead whose own
ask was not this room's ruling is still refused as Image 1). Image 3 is the row-38 edge strip from
the facing it continues, where that facing abuts this one.

**`depends_on` keeps row 38's meaning** — non-null means THIS ASK MUST WAIT, because what it
continues has no picture yet — so every reader that already routes on it keeps working. What is
new is that an indoor facing can now carry one. The lead and the whole order are recorded per room
in the manifest as `room_order`.

**The standing order.** Verified, and it did NOT already say this: `tools/baton-watch.sh`'s `ORDER`
tells the seat to paint every roll whose candidate is missing, with no mention of waiting, and its
`owed` count counts blocked rolls as owed — so under this row three quarters of the manor would be
painted out of order and the baton would nudge for it. Both are fixed here.

## (2) Windows are detected after the fact

**`design/plan-draft/measured/window_measure.py`**, beside `door_measure.py` and built the same
way: it knows the frame's scale and floor line and nothing about which window is which, it
enumerates every glazed opening it can see, and it refuses nothing — the plan lives in
`tools/promote-backdrop.mjs` and so do the refusals.

The discriminator is the leaded lattice. A window in this corpus is a bright rectangle between the
plan's sill (0.90 m) and head (2.00 m) bands, and so is a limewashed wall in sunlight; what only
the window has is a fine periodic edge signature — the quarries of a leaded light — so the reading
carries a `lattice` score and the candidate is admitted on brightness AND lattice together.

**`meta.windows`** is written by `promote-backdrop.mjs` in the same shape as `meta.openings`
(`id/kind/x/y/w/h/measured`), with a `[row42:window.*]` clause family: the plan-ruled-missing case
GATES (a window the plan rules that the painting does not show), the painting-only case is
RECORDED (row 27's door rule read in both directions).

**The renderer** may carry windows in its aperture list as non-exit apertures of kind `window`,
opt-in only, because the page turns every aperture into a `go` target and a window is not a way
through. Nothing new is drawn.

## What the build settled that the plan left open

- **The lattice is the JUNIOR discriminator on this store, and it is said so in the file.** Measured
  over all 24 promoted window walls and the 30 promoted walls the plan rules no window on, the LIFT
  separates 54–147 against 21–85 and does most of the work; the lattice removes two of the four
  bright pieces of wall the lift alone admits, at the cost of one painted window. Both are kept
  because they fail differently and row 37 is about to make brightness meaningless.
- **A window is mullioned and a window floats.** Neither was in the plan. One opening reads as two or
  three bright runs with a bar between them (merged under 0.25 m), and the paintings draw their sills
  and heads outside the plan's ruled band — so the band bounds the SEARCH and the light's own edges
  give `y` and `h`. Three overlays read by eye are what found both.
- **Two clauses, not one:** `[row42:window.unpainted]` and `[row42:window.painted_width]`, the second
  being row 27's `door.painted_width` on a wider band because what is measured is the LIGHT and the
  plan rules the OPENING.
- **`meta.windows` is written only where the MEASUREMENT carries a window reading**, so every wall
  already in the store promotes byte-identically and `fixtures.spec`'s promotion-staleness case stays
  green without re-deriving 61 metas (which is a write to `backdrops/`, fenced for this seat). The
  sweep closes the gap: `row23_run.py` runs `window_reading` on every reading from here on, the list
  of walls the clause cannot see is `window_calibration.json`, and it can only shrink. Logged as an
  OPEN miss in `misses.jsonl`.
- **`door_measure.py` was refactored** onto the shared `measure_lib.maximally_stable_runs` so the two
  detectors read one sweep. Its control frame still reads 673..861.

## (3) Leaves are placed

**Where the binding lives.** On the ENTITY, as `kind` + `fills`, and resolved by
`groundplane.leafFor` — beside `openingFor`, which is already the one home of what an exit's `via`
names. The inverse (an opening in `plan.json` naming its entity) exists and stays for the row-2
world; a second home for the same question is how the renderer, the harness, the page and the
validator would come to disagree about which doorways have doors in them. Writing `entity` onto 26
manor openings was the alternative and it moves the drawn digest of the approved drawing.

**Where the sprite draws.** `groundplane.apertureRect` — the measured rect for what the entity
fills, or null. `layout` fits to it on both axes, which is why the layout entry gains `fx` beside
`f`. Fitted only where the rectangle is MEASURED, because a derived opening is the plan's rectangle
wearing the painting's field names.

**What null means, per kind, and it is not the same answer.** A door falls back to §4 placement (row
2 unchanged, every unpromoted wall). A casement is not drawn: the promotion's own
`window.unpainted` clause refuses a casement on unmeasured paint, and the renderer must not do by
default what the pipeline refuses to promote.

**The light hook.** `light_state` on every aperture entry and every fitted layout entry. Row 42
draws no lighting; row 37 reads the bit.

**The library.** Two new placeholder records with `placeholder: true` and their painter ask named on
the record. `door-plank` is NOT reused: §11 authors it symmetric so one image serves both rooms, and
a leaf that swings to its hinge side needs a hinge side.

## Edges

- `backdrops/` and `run-state.json` are read, never written. The window demo therefore runs the real
  promotion in a staged copy of the tree and captures from there.
- `door-plank`, `fixtures/demo-study` and every unmeasured wall in the manor render byte-identically.
- `tools/validate-plan.mjs`'s "no §6 record" clause discriminated on `worldLocation.size > 0` — any

  the discriminator became "does this world furnish the plan"; without it, hanging one leaf reported
  four unfurnished plan footprints as missing records.
- `stagingDivergence` needs the world to see `fills`, or an aperture-bound placement reads as one the
  plan holds no position for and refuses the bake.

## What the build settled that the plan left open

- **The demo doorway is `op22`, not the row's own suggestion.** Every measured door rectangle was
  compared against the dark run its own painting draws: 23 of 27 agree within 0.94–1.02×, and four do
  not — `great_hall/S` 0.65×, `buttery_pantry/S` 1.95×, `dining_parlour/N` 1.67×, `privy_garden/S`
  1.21×. `great_hall/S` was the row's suggested wall and its leaf faithfully fills two thirds of the
  doorway the painter drew. `op22` is measured on BOTH its facings and at two different scales, so
  one leaf in two painted frames is what the demo shows instead. **The four flagged walls are an
  open `door_measure.py` reading**, recorded in `design/architecture.md` and the demo README.
- **`solar/S` and `long_gallery/E` cannot carry a demo casement.** Both were named in the handoff and
  both REFUSE promotion under `[row42:window.unpainted]` — the plan rules four windows on each and
  the painting shows three. `kitchen/E` (1 ruled, 1 painted, lattice 0.493, confidence 0.91) is the
  wall the demo uses.
- **The glass is a wash, not a fill.** The first casement painted an opaque leaded pane and put a
  grey slab over a painting that had already drawn daylight through leaded glass. The came lattice
  and the ironwork are opaque; the quarries are a 0.14-alpha wash. The painter ask says so as a rule
  rather than as a note.
